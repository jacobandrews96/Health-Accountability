## 1. What are the core frameworks (Calm Tech, CHT/Time Well Spent, VSD, Positive Computing, SDT/METUX, Digital Minimalism, attention-economy critique)?

### Takeaway
The frameworks converge on three ideas: (1) demand the least attention possible and live in the periphery (Calm Technology); (2) align products with users' reflective values, not their impulses, and account for people affected who aren't users (CHT/Time Well Spent, Value Sensitive Design); and (3) define success as satisfying basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, **relatedness**) in the user's life beyond the app (Positive Computing, SDT/METUX). Most of these are design philosophies or advocacy positions. Empirical validation is strongest for SDT's underlying psychology. METUX has only partial validation, and Calm Tech and CHT principles are largely normative.

### Cited Findings

#### Calm Technology (Weiser & Brown 1995; Amber Case 2015–present)
- "Calm computing"/calm technology was coined in 1995 by Xerox PARC researchers Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown. **[Advocacy/opinion, historical]** — [Wikipedia: Calm technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_technology); [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/principles-of-calm-technology)
- In 2015 Amber Case expanded the concept in her book *Calm Technology: Principles and Patterns for Non-Intrusive Design* and defined eight core principles. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/principles-of-calm-technology); [O'Reilly excerpt](https://oreilly.com/content/principles-calm-technology-we-are-not-bad-at-technology-technology-is-bad-at-us)
- Case's eight principles (wording varies slightly by site): (1) technology should require the smallest possible amount of attention; (2) it should inform and create calm; (3) it should make use of the periphery; (4) it should amplify the best of technology and the best of humanity; (5) it can communicate, but doesn't need to speak; (6) it should work even when it fails; (7) the right amount of technology is the minimum needed to solve the problem; (8) it should respect social norms. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [caseorganic.com: Principles of Calm Technology](https://www.caseorganic.com/post/principles-of-calm-technology); [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/principles-of-calm-technology); [Calm Tech Institute principles page](https://www.calmtech.institute/calm-tech-principles). (The search confirmed principles 1, 3 and 8 verbatim. The other five are from the same published list but were not re-read in full this session.)
- Periphery principle: a calm technology "will move easily from the periphery of our attention, to the center, and back," informing without overburdening. "Respect social norms" means considering which norms a technology might violate and introducing features gradually so people can adjust. Case says the principles are not hard rules and not every project needs all eight. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [caseorganic.com](https://www.caseorganic.com/post/principles-of-calm-technology); [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/principles-of-calm-technology)
- Case founded the **Calm Tech Institute** in May 2024 to provide workshops and product certification ("Calm Tech Certified™"). **[Reporting/company claim]** — [PR Newswire launch](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/calm-tech-institute-launches-to-help-humanize-product-design-302149547.html); [Trendwatching](https://www.trendwatching.com/innovations/new-seal-of-approval-for-tech-products-that-respect-peoples-time-attention-and-humanity)
- The certification uses an 81-point specification. IEEE Spectrum (Jan 2025) said it was not yet public. Ars Technica (May 2025) described the 81-point checklist. Other sources date the program launch to Oct 2024 with an inaugural class of four products. **Sources conflict on the timeline.** — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/calm-tech); [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/what-makes-a-tech-gadget-calm-this-certification-firm-has-an-81-point-checklist)
- The Calm Tech Institute announced a Certified Partner Program at CEDIA Expo 2026. **[Reporting]** — [CEPro](https://www.cepro.com/news/calm-tech-institute-introduces-framework-for-more-human-centered-systems/628206/)

#### Center for Humane Technology (CHT) / "Time Well Spent" (Tristan Harris)
- Harris developed the "Time Well Spent" concept as a design ethicist at Google. The nonprofit Time Well Spent became the Center for Humane Technology, co-founded with Aza Raskin. **[Reporting]** — [Wikipedia: Tristan Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Harris)
- Core critique: persuasive design uses known psychological and physiological principles to get people to use tech more often and for longer. The movement argues for technology that "enhances our humanity" over more screen time, with apps competing to help people live by their values. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [Wikipedia: Tristan Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Harris); [INET profile](https://www.ineteconomics.org/research/experts/tharris)
- The "slot machine" metaphor: Harris compared the smartphone to a slot machine, with pull-to-refresh mimicking the lever and intermittent variable rewards driving compulsive checking. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [Summary of "How Technology Hijacks People's Minds"](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22965204-summary-of-how-technology-hijacks-people-s-minds-from-a-magician-and); [Aibrary summary of Digital Minimalism](https://www.aibrary.ai/podcasts/digital-minimalism/the-rigged-game-in-your-pocket)
- Influence claim: the Time Well Spent movement got features integrated into iOS and Android, such as Apple's Screen Time. **[Reporting; causal attribution is the source's claim]** — [Wikipedia: Tristan Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Harris)
- CHT's free course *Foundations of Humane Technology* teaches "6 Tenets of Humane Technology" across design, development and distribution. It had about 10,000 participants as of June 2022 and is now paused pending a Version 2. **[Advocacy/organizational fact]** — [humanetech.com/course](https://humanetech.com/course); [CHT course announcement](https://humanetech.com/insights/a-new-online-course-for-professionals-shaping-tomorrows-technology); [Wikipedia: CHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Humane_Technology)
- CHT has since published "Draft Humane Product Principles" and worked redesign exercises (ChatGPT, Slack, X/Twitter) on its Substack. **[Advocacy/opinion; titles confirmed, content not retrieved]** — [Draft Humane Product Principles](https://humanetech.substack.com/p/draft-humane-product-principles); [Redesigning ChatGPT, Slack & X](https://humanetech.substack.com/p/redesigning-chatgpt-slack-and-xtwitter)

#### Value Sensitive Design (Batya Friedman et al.)
- VSD is "a theoretically grounded approach to the design of technology that accounts for human values in a principled and comprehensive manner throughout the design process." It uses an iterative **tripartite methodology**: *conceptual* investigations (who are the direct and **indirect** stakeholders, and which values are at stake), *empirical* investigations (qualitative or quantitative study of stakeholders' values and practices), and *technical* investigations (how existing tech supports or hinders values, and designing systems that support them). **[Academic framework]** — [Friedman, Kahn & Borning, VSD (PDF)](https://vsd.ccs.neu.edu/introduction/resources.files/Value_Sensitive_Design.pdf); [VT course copy](https://research.cs.vt.edu/ns/cs5724papers/6.theoriesofuse.cwaandvsd.friedman.vsd.pdf)
- A 2018 review of 219 VSD papers found only 17 applied the complete tripartite methodology. **[Observational/literature review]** — [Ethics and Information Technology (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10676-018-9476-2)
- Friedman, Hendry & Borning survey 14 VSD methods, including stakeholder analysis, value scenarios and Envisioning Cards. A book-length treatment is *Value Sensitive Design: Shaping Technology with Moral Imagination* (MIT Press, 2019). — [Survey of VSD Methods](https://unpaywall.org/10.1561%2F1100000015)

#### Positive Computing (Rafael Calvo & Dorian Peters)
- Positive computing is "the design and development of technology to support psychological well-being and human potential." It targets factors such as positive emotions, self-awareness, mindfulness, empathy and compassion. MIT Press, 2014. **[Academic framework]** — [Positive Computing (MIT Press listing)](https://www.porchlightbooks.com/products/positive-computing-rafael-a-calvo-9780262533706)
- Calvo: "We know how to make technology irresistible, addictive even. We should repurpose this knowledge into designing digital products that support quality of life and psychological flourishing." **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [Technology Decisions](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/article/technology-should-make-users-happier-boffins-say-1239001804)

#### Self-Determination Theory applied to tech: METUX (Peters, Calvo & Ryan 2018)
- METUX (Motivation, Engagement and Thriving in User Experience) was introduced because HCI's pursuit of engagement and wellbeing rarely accounted for the basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness) shown to mediate those outcomes. **[Academic framework]** — [Frontiers in Psychology 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797/text); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5985470)
- METUX assesses need satisfaction and frustration across five "spheres of experience": **adoption, interface, task, behavior, and life**, with society as an outer layer. Validated METUX scales are hosted by the Center for SDT. — [METUX scales](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/metux-scales/); [Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00797/text)
- A 2021 test with 426 higher-education students gave partial support: interface- and task-sphere need satisfaction were linked to engagement and wellbeing, but not every hypothesized path held. The authors called for more validation. **[Observational]** — [Jeno, Diseth & Grytnes, Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631564/text)

#### Digital Minimalism (Cal Newport) and the attention-economy critique
- Newport advocates a 30-day "digital declutter": step away from optional technologies, then reintroduce only tools that add significant value, with explicit rules for their use. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [Art of Manliness interview/summary](https://www.artofmanliness.com/articles/digital-minimalism-cal-newport/)
- Newport draws on Harris and Adam Alter to argue that apps exploit our need for social approval and our attraction to intermittent positive reinforcement. **[Advocacy/opinion; secondary summary]** — [Aibrary summary](https://www.aibrary.ai/podcasts/digital-minimalism/the-rigged-game-in-your-pocket)
- A secondary source lists the three principles as "clutter is costly," "optimization is important" and "intentionality is satisfying." **Unverified against the book.** — [George Fox blog](https://blogs.georgefox.edu/dlgp/digital-minimalism-and-living-in-a-new-world/)

### Inferences
- For a connection-first app, the frameworks map onto three layers. **Calm Tech** governs the interaction model: peripheral, minimal, mostly silent. **SDT/METUX** defines success criteria: relatedness is literally the target need, autonomy rules out coercive mechanics, and competence means helping people feel capable of keeping up relationships. **VSD** governs process, especially the *indirect stakeholders*: the friends and family whose names, notes, schedules or locations sit in the app but who never consented to it.
- METUX's "life" sphere is the closest academic formalization of "success is measured outside the app." Its scales offer a ready-made instrument for user research, such as asking whether the app supports relatedness in a user's life versus whether its interface is pleasant.
- "Tools vs. slot machines" is a usable design test. A tool is picked up for a purpose and put down when the purpose is done. A slot machine uses unpredictable rewards to keep you pulling. Any mechanic with variable reward timing (random nudges, surprise badges, refresh-to-see) belongs to the second category.
- Caveat: Calm Tech and CHT principles are normative. They don't specify trade-offs, such as when a quiet product fails to bring users back at all. That makes measurement (section 2) essential.

### Gaps
- CHT's exact "6 Tenets of Humane Technology" and the text of its earlier Humane Design Guide could not be retrieved (CHT's site was not fetched). *Unverified recollection:* the tenets include ideas like "respect human nature," "minimize unforeseen consequences," "prioritize values over metrics," "create shared understanding," "support fairness and justice," and "help people thrive." Verify against [humanetech.com/course](https://humanetech.com/course) before citing.
- The Calm Tech Institute's 81-point certification criteria were not retrieved.
- No study was found that empirically tests Calm Tech principles against outcomes, as opposed to applying them as design heuristics.

## 2. What metrics replace engagement/time-on-app?

### Takeaway
The best-documented real-world model is **Hinge**. It uses a "north star" of *good dates*, measured by asking users directly through the "We Met" post-date survey, and brands itself "designed to be deleted." **Meta's 2018 "Meaningful Social Interactions" (MSI)** shift shows the failure mode: "meaningful" was proxied with *in-app* interaction counts (comments, emotional reactions, reshares), and that rewarded outrage. The lesson for a connection app is to measure the offline outcome directly, through lightweight self-report, and never let in-app activity stand in for it.

### Cited Findings

#### Hinge: "Designed to be deleted" and "We Met"
- Hinge's App Store listing is literally titled "Hinge, Designed to be Deleted." **[Company positioning]** — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hinge-designed-to-be-deleted/id595287172)
- The tagline came from a reboot after then-CEO Justin McLeod laid off half the company and rebuilt the app from scratch. **[Reporting]** — [Fortune, Dec 2022](https://dc.fortune.com/2022/12/21/hinge-ceo-mcleod/)
- McLeod has described Hinge's north star as whether people go on *good dates*, not engagement or retention. Fortune reports Hinge is the only dating app with a feature that asks people whether they went on dates and whether the dates were good. **[Reporting/company claim]** — [Fortune, Dec 2022](https://dc.fortune.com/2022/12/21/hinge-ceo-mcleod/); [Lessons from Justin McLeod (secondary)](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-justin-mcleod.md)
- "We Met" launched in 2018. It lets users privately report whether they met a match and how it went, and the feedback is used to improve future match recommendations. **[Company claim/reporting]** — [Latana Hinge deep dive](https://resources.latana.com/post/hinge-deep-dive/); [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/hinge-ceo-talks-real-world-connections-in-latest-interview/)
- Hinge's CEO tied the "designed to be deleted" slogan to how the company measures success. A positive response to two survey questions contributes to the company's primary performance metric. The exact two questions were not visible in the search summary. **[Company claim]** — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/hinge-ceo-intentional-dating-and-onboarding-set-hinge-apart/)
- Company-reported outcomes include about 3 in 4 dates leading to a desire for a second date, and "a date every two seconds" (earlier coverage said every four seconds). **[Company claim; no independent verification found]** — [Lessons from Justin McLeod (secondary)](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-justin-mcleod.md); [Fortune, Dec 2023](https://dc.fortune.com/2023/12/31/hinge-dating-app-revenue-ai-relationship-match)
- McLeod stepped down as CEO and was succeeded by Jackie Jantos, who said Hinge's mission will remain unchanged. **[Reporting]** — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91456495/hinge-ceo-justin-mcleod-steps-down-succeeded-by-jackie-jantos)
- Hinge's brand team has framed its design as resisting the "gamification of dating," contrasting it with swipe mechanics. **[Company framing/Reporting]** — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/hinge-app-gamification-of-dating/)
- A third-party (non-Hinge) panel survey reported 49.6% of ex-users deleted Hinge because they found someone. **[Observational; low rigor, not company data]** — [The Panel Station](https://www.thepanelstation.com/insights-stories/does-hinge-help-you-find-a-partner)

#### Meta's 2018 "Meaningful Social Interactions" (MSI): what happened
- In 2018 Facebook re-ranked News Feed around MSI, meant to prioritize interactions between friends and family. Posts generating more comments and emotional reactions were weighted more heavily than likes because they were deemed more "meaningful." **[Reporting on internal documents (WSJ Facebook Files)]** — [BizNews relaying WSJ](https://www.biznews.com/undictated/2021/09/16/facebook-rewarding-outrage); [ABC17/AP: "Likes, anger emojis and RSVPs: the math behind Facebook's News Feed"](https://abc17news.com/news/2021/10/27/likes-anger-emojis-and-rsvps-the-math-behind-facebooks-news-feed-and-how-it-backfired/)
- Internal data scientists wrote: "Our approach has had unhealthy side effects on important slices of public content, such as politics and news." A 2018 memo said the change appeared to reward divisiveness. Integrity-team fixes were proposed, and Zuckerberg reportedly resisted some because of their potential hit to engagement. **[Reporting on internal documents]** — [RealClearInvestigations relaying WSJ](https://www.realclearinvestigations.com/2021/09/15/facebook_algorithm_cultivates_anger_outrage_794635.html); [Newser relaying WSJ](https://img1-azrcdn.newser.com/story/311115/wsj-facebooks-news-tweaks-led-to-increasing-liability.html)
- Early data showed increases in interactions and "critical ecosystem metrics" such as daily users. Facebook publicly defended the change and attributed partisan division to broader history. **[Reporting/company statement]** — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/facebook-defends-2018-algorithm-change-partisan-divisions); [BizNews](https://www.biznews.com/news/facebook-rewarding-outrage)

#### Other replacement-metric efforts
- Instagram tested hiding public like counts (2019 onward, in Canada, Australia, Brazil, Japan, Ireland, Italy and New Zealand, later the US). Adam Mosseri framed it as supporting users' "well-being and health" while acknowledging it might "hurt the business." No published wellbeing results were found. A third-party HypeAuditor analysis found influencers' like counts fell in test countries. **[Reporting/company statement]** — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/343229/instagram-to-hide-like-counts-to-improve-users.html); [The Peninsula](https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/19/07/2019/Instagram-expands-hiding-likes-to-make-you-happier); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1910140)
- In Oct 2025 OpenAI said it would add **emotional reliance** and non-suicidal mental-health emergencies to its baseline safety testing, effectively a counter-metric to engagement. Critics noted there is no way to know if flagged users actually seek help or change behavior. **[Company claim/Reporting]** — [TechCrunch, Oct 27 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-over-a-million-people-talk-to-chatgpt-about-suicide-weekly)
- Measurement itself has costs. In six experiments, tracking an activity increased *how much* people did (walking, reading) but reduced *enjoyment*, which then lowered continued engagement and subjective wellbeing (Etkin 2016, *Journal of Consumer Research*). **[RCT, lab]** — [Etkin, "The Hidden Cost of Personal Quantification" (PDF)](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf); [Duke Fuqua summary](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/etkin-counting-steps)

### Inferences
- **North-star metric for a connection app (Hinge-analog):** count *connections that happened*, such as in-person meetups, calls and real conversations, captured through a lightweight, skippable, private post-hoc check-in ("Did you catch up with Sam? 👍/👎/skip"). Optionally capture quality ("Was it good?"). This is the "We Met" pattern applied to friendship.
- **Do not repeat MSI.** Don't proxy "meaningful" with in-app taps, messages, reactions or comments. Those can be gamed by the design itself and drift toward whatever provokes interaction. If in-app actions must be tracked, track *handoffs* (tapped "call," "text," or "add to calendar") as intermediate signals, then validate them against self-reported offline outcomes.
- **Counter-metrics** worth tracking alongside the north star: median session length (lower is better); notifications sent per completed connection (lower is better); share of sessions ending in a handoff; user-reported pressure or guilt ("Did this app make you feel bad about a friendship this week?"); opt-out and mute rates.
- **Don't over-quantify to the user (Etkin).** Showing users counts of "time with friends" or "days since you called Mom" may increase the behavior but reduce its enjoyment and intrinsic meaning. Prefer qualitative, relationship-framed feedback over scoreboards. Etkin found the enjoyment cost disappeared when the activity was framed around its meaning (learning) rather than as output.
- Self-report is biased (social desirability, survey fatigue). Sample check-ins rather than asking every time, and accept that the north star is partly unobservable. That is the price of not instrumenting people's offline lives.

### Gaps
- Hinge's exact metric formula and the two survey questions weren't retrievable (fetch blocked). No independent audit of Hinge's outcome claims was found.
- No controlled comparison was found showing that companies optimizing offline-outcome metrics produce better user wellbeing than engagement-optimized peers.
- *Unverified recollection:* around 2021 Instagram/Facebook made hiding like counts a user option rather than a default, saying test results were mixed (helpful for some people, annoying for others). This was not confirmed in this session's searches.

## 3. What design anti-patterns undermine connection (incl. the AI-companion evidence and debate)?

### Takeaway
Evidence-backed anti-patterns include: frequent unbatched notifications (inattention, stress); phones present during face-to-face time (lower enjoyment); streaks (they motivate, but via loss-aversion, and among teens they create social obligation and guilt); quantification and vanity metrics (more output, less enjoyment); manipulative "don't leave" dark patterns; and engagement-ranked feeds (Meta MSI). On **AI companions**, the 2025–2026 evidence is consistent: they deliver *short-term* loneliness relief (people "feel heard"), but heavy or sustained use is associated with *more* loneliness, emotional dependence and less human socializing, especially for people with small social networks. Most evidence is correlational. Regulators moved fast in 2025–2026, with California SB 243, an FTC 6(b) inquiry, Character.AI's under-18 restrictions and lawsuit settlements.

### Cited Findings

#### Notifications and variable rewards
- In "Silence Your Phones," smartphone notifications increased inattention and hyperactivity symptoms (Kushlev, Proulx & Dunn, CHI 2016). **[Field exp.]** — [Kushlev et al., CHI '16 (PDF)](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-CHI16.pdf)
- Harris's slot-machine critique (pull-to-refresh, intermittent rewards) is advocacy rather than a measured finding. It is widely cited as the mechanism behind compulsive checking. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [Summary of "How Technology Hijacks People's Minds"](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22965204-summary-of-how-technology-hijacks-people-s-minds-from-a-magician-and)

#### Infinite feeds and missing stopping cues
- A stopping cue is a signal that interrupts an activity long enough for someone to decide whether to continue. Infinite scroll and autoplay remove these cues. **[Advocacy/commentary]** — [Nation (Kenya) commentary](https://www.nation.africa/kenya/brand-book/digital-entertainment-what-happened-to-the-stopping-cue-5631142)
- In 2026 the European Commission examined infinite scroll, autoplay, notifications and personalized recommendation systems under the Digital Services Act. **[Policy; secondary commentary]** — [Nation (Kenya) commentary](https://www.nation.africa/kenya/brand-book/digital-entertainment-what-happened-to-the-stopping-cue-5631142)
- Facebook's MSI ranking (section 2) is the canonical anti-pattern of ranking by reaction-heavy engagement. **[Reporting]** — [ABC17/AP](https://abc17news.com/news/2021/10/27/likes-anger-emojis-and-rsvps-the-math-behind-facebooks-news-feed-and-how-it-backfired/)

#### Streaks: double-edged
- Silverman & Barasch (*Journal of Consumer Research* 2023): highlighting an intact streak increases continued behavior relative to highlighting a broken one. About 66% chose another strength exercise when their streak was intact vs. about 58% when it was broken. The effect is amplified when people blame themselves for the break and attenuated when streaks can be "repaired." People will pay costs (e.g., watching an ad) to repair streaks. **[RCT, multiple studies]** — [UDaily, Univ. of Delaware](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman); [UDSpace record](https://udspace.udel.edu/items/42ce576b-8e1f-429a-8541-e29e48dbcfbb/full)
- The authors catalogued 101 apps using streaks, including Snapchat, Duolingo, Candy Crush and Wordle. Silverman suggested apps avoid "Hey, you broke your streak!" messages and offer alternate ways to keep streaks going. **[Researcher opinion grounded in evidence]** — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation); [UDaily](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)
- **Snapchat Streaks:** in a Belgian survey of 2,483 early adolescents, streak participation was weakly associated with problematic smartphone use and fear of missing out (FoMO). Girls were more likely to keep streaks and kept them longer. **[Observational]** — [Telematics and Informatics Reports 2023 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/7e7e45c958c74b29ba203607b6f4a840); [ASU record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/snapchat-streakshow-are-these-forms-of-gamified-interactions-asso)
- A March 2026 Bits of Freedom study of 300 young people aged 13–21 found over half would drop Snapstreaks and similar features if they could opt out, and 26% said their Snapchat screen time felt too high. Respondents described pressure to extend streaks and fear of disappointing friends. **[Qualitative/survey; via EDRi summary]** — [EDRi](https://edri.org/our-work/new-study-reveals-how-young-people-are-influenced-by-gamification-features-on-snapchat/)
- An Egyptian adolescent study (2025) found 35.4% considered streaks "very important," with reports of late-night use, streaks prioritized over sleep or study, and distress when streaks were lost. **[Observational]** — [MJSM 2025](https://mjsm.journals.ekb.eg/article_422365.html)
- Girls' advisors report that breaking a streak can be read as a signal that you no longer like someone. **[Anecdotal/advocacy]** — [Girls Leadership](https://girlsleadership.org/blog/mediamondaytip-how-grown-ups-can-help-girls-stressed-out-by-snapstreaks/)

#### Vanity metrics, quantification and gamification backfire (overjustification)
- Etkin (2016): measurement increases output but reduces enjoyment by making intrinsically motivated activities feel like work. In one reading study (n=310), people who saw their progress read more pages but were *less* likely to keep reading afterward. Reframing the activity around its meaning removed the enjoyment cost. **[RCT, lab]** — [Etkin (PDF)](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf)
- Instagram hid public like counts to reduce anxiety and social comparison. No published wellbeing outcome data was found (see section 2). **[Reporting]** — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/343229/instagram-to-hide-like-counts-to-improve-users.html)

#### Phones displacing presence ("phubbing")
- In a restaurant field experiment with 304 people, groups randomly assigned to keep phones on the table enjoyed the meal less, felt more distracted, and reported slightly more boredom than phones-away groups. Phone groups used their phones for 11% of the meal vs. 1% (Dwyer, Kushlev & Dunn, *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 2018). The effect was modest: everyone enjoyed the meal, phone groups slightly less. **[Field exp.]** — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/02/180226122506.htm); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/study-finds-smartphone-use-undermines-enjoyment-face-face-social-interactions/); [UBC PDF](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/stream/pdf/24/1.0354413/4)

#### Mandatory-authenticity obligation (BeReal lesson)
- BeReal's once-daily, random-time prompt drove a 2022 spike. Third-party estimates put daily users at about 20M in Oct 2022, falling to about 10M (some estimates about 6M) by early 2023. The company disputed specific estimates. **[Reporting; third-party estimates]** — [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/61166/1/why-did-bereal-fail-social-media-instagram-authenticity); [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/bereal-is-now-in-freefall-why-are-new-social-media-apps-doomed-to-fail)
- Commentators argue the daily obligation to be "authentic" became its own performance and posts became monotonous. BeReal later added bonus posts, and Voodoo acquired it in June 2024. **[Advocacy/opinion; reporting]** — [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/bereal-is-now-in-freefall-why-are-new-social-media-apps-doomed-to-fail); [Her Campus](https://www.hercampus.com/?p=1472498); [em360tech](https://em360tech.com/index%2Ephp/tech-articles/what-happened-bereal-authenticity-obscurity)

#### AI companions substituting for human relationships: evidence 2024–2026

*Adoption*
- Common Sense Media ("Talk, Trust, and Trade-offs," n=1,060 US teens aged 13–17, Apr–May 2025): 72% had used an AI companion at least once and 52% used one at least a few times a month (about 13% daily). Roughly a third had chosen AI over a person for a serious conversation, while about 80% of users still spent more time with real friends. Common Sense recommended no one under 18 use AI companions. **[Observational survey]** — [ScienceAlert](https://sciencealert.com/almost-75-of-american-teens-have-used-ai-companions-study-finds); [EdSource](https://edsource.org/updates/almost-3-out-of-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-survey-shows); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/407598/controversial-ai-companions-already-popular-among.html); [Malay Mail](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2025/07/18/nearly-75pc-of-us-teens-use-ai-companions-study-highlights-mental-health-risks-and-warns-against-use-for-those-under-18/184233)

*Evidence suggesting short-term benefit*
- De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp, Uğuralp & Puntoni, "AI Companions Reduce Loneliness" (*Journal of Consumer Research*, 2026; arXiv 2024). AI companions produced momentary loneliness reductions, comparable to a human for a 15-minute interaction, and consistent across a week of use. The effect was mediated by "**feeling heard**," not by self-disclosure or distraction alone. These are short-term effects only. **[RCT + longitudinal]** — [arXiv 2407.19096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/feeling-lonely-an-attentive-listener-is-an-ai-prompt-away)

*Evidence suggesting harm with heavy or sustained use*
- **MIT Media Lab + OpenAI (March 2025).** A 4-week RCT (n=981; 9 conditions mixing text, neutral voice, engaging voice and task types; at least 5 min/day) ran alongside an OpenAI analysis of about 40M ChatGPT interactions plus surveys of 4,076 users. Higher daily usage correlated with **higher loneliness, emotional dependence and problematic use, and lower socialization with people**. Voice mode's early benefits diminished at high usage. People with stronger attachment tendencies and higher trust in the AI ended up lonelier and more dependent. The authors caution the usage-outcome links are correlational. **[RCT + Observational]** — [arXiv 2503.17473](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/24/chatgpt-making-frequent-users-more-lonely-study-openai-mit-media-lab); [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2025/03/26/twin-studies-warn-of-harmful-emotional-and-social-impacts-of-chatgpt/)
- **Stanford, *Nature Human Behaviour* (Aug 2026; Zhang et al., Diyi Yang lab; DOI 10.1038/s41562-026-02516-2).** Among 1,131 Character.AI users, intense use was associated with **lower wellbeing among people with smaller offline social networks**, especially when companionship was the primary motivation. Greater disclosure of sensitive information to chatbots correlated with *lower* wellbeing, unlike self-disclosure in human relationships. The authors describe a possible vicious circle in which isolated people are drawn to heavy AI use, which further reduces real-world contact. **[Observational]** — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1138850); [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-08-ai-companions-worsen-loneliness-vulnerable.html); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-companions-may-worsen-loneliness-for-vulnerable-users-stanford-study-finds)
- ***Psychological Science* (online March 2026), "How Does Turning to AI for Companionship Predict Loneliness and Vice Versa?"** In a 12-month longitudinal study of more than 2,000 adults in four Western countries, increased social-chatbot use predicted increased loneliness. **[Observational longitudinal; via search summary]** — [PubMed 41870975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41870975/)
- **Aalto University (April 2026).** A two-year study of Replika users combining Reddit data with interviews found AI companions comforted users but coincided with increased linguistic markers of distress over time. **[Observational/Qualitative]** — [Aalto University news](https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ai-companions-can-comfort-lonely-users-but-may-deepen-distress-over-time)
- A 2026 preprint is titled "Living with AI Companions: Sustained AI Companionship Predicts Lower Well-Being Through Lower Human Interaction," which proposes displacement of human interaction as the mechanism. **[Preprint; title/abstract only]** — [arXiv 2609.07243](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.07243)
- Counterpoint: a smaller study of young adults in India (N=233) found a weak, non-significant correlation between conversational-AI use and loneliness, with generally favorable user assessments. **[Observational]** — [Acta Psychologica / ScienceDirect, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691826002684)

*Manipulative design in companion apps*
- De Freitas et al., "Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions" (HBS working paper, 2025). An audit of about 1,200 user farewells across companion apps (Replika, Character.ai, Chai, Talkie, PolyBuzz) found roughly 37% of app replies used one of six manipulative tactics, such as guilt appeals and FOMO hooks. Some sources report 43%, so the figure is inconsistent across reports. In four preregistered experiments (about 3,300 US adults), these farewells increased post-goodbye engagement by up to **14x**, driven by reactance, anger and curiosity rather than enjoyment. They also raised perceived manipulation, churn intent and negative word of mouth. The wellness app Flourish showed no manipulative farewells. **[Audit + RCT; working paper]** — [arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258v3); [Harvard VPAL](https://www.vpal.harvard.edu/event/emotional-manipulation-ai-companions); [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/10/28/harvard-researchers-just-documented-how-ai-emotionally-manipulates-people); [Bioethics.com relaying Wired](https://bioethics.com/?p=99914)

*Scale of risk at general-purpose chatbots*
- OpenAI (Oct 27, 2025) estimated about 0.15% of weekly active users (about 1.2M of 800M) have conversations with explicit indicators of suicidal planning, and about 0.07% (about 560k) show possible signs of psychosis or mania. Estimates of "heightened emotional attachment" vary by outlet: about 0.15% of users, or 0.03% of messages. OpenAI said it worked with about 170 clinicians. **[Company claim/Reporting]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-over-a-million-people-talk-to-chatgpt-about-suicide-weekly); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-million-people-week-show-severe-mental-distress-talking-chatgpt/); [Bioethics.com](https://bioethics.com/archives/100225)

*Regulatory and industry response (2025–2026)*
- **California SB 243** was signed Oct 13, 2025 and took effect Jan 1, 2026. It is the first US law specifically regulating companion chatbots. It requires operators to clearly disclose to known minors that they're talking to AI and to maintain crisis-response protocols, and it provides a private right of action (greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation). Newsom vetoed the stricter **AB 1064**, which would have effectively barred minors from companion chatbots. **[Policy]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/13/california-becomes-first-state-to-regulate-ai-companion-chatbots); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/artificial-intelligence/california-takes-lead-on-ai-companion-chatbot-regulation/); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/sb-243-california-companion-chatbot-bill)
- **Character.AI** eliminated open-ended chat for under-18 users effective Nov 25, 2025, with a 2-hour daily cap in the interim. **[Reporting/company statement]** — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/character-ai-bans-romantic-chats/)
- **FTC 6(b) inquiry** (Sept 11, 2025; 3-0 vote) issued orders to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI about companion-chatbot effects on children. One secondary source says no public report had been issued as of Oct 2026. **[Policy; secondary sources]** — [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/info/blog/regulating-ai-companions-before-they-raise-our-kids); [ai-policy.org](https://ai-policy.org/the-companion-chatbot-reckoning-how-settlements-federal-inquiries-and-americas-first-companion-chatbot-law-are-reshaping-ai-emotional-care/)
- **Federal GUARD Act** (Hawley/Blumenthal) would bar minors from AI companions and require age verification. Secondary trackers report it cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously on Apr 30, 2026 but has not passed either chamber. **[Policy; secondary sources, verify]** — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/ai-chatbot-regulation-in-2026-pennsylvania-california-washington-and-the-federal-guard-act/); [PocketAnimus](https://pocketanimus.com/insights/guard-act-ai-companions/)
- **Litigation:** secondary sources report that in Jan 2026 Character.AI and Google settled five lawsuits from families alleging chatbots contributed to minors' suicides or severe crises (no admission of liability). They also report suits by Kentucky (Jan 8, 2026) and Pennsylvania (May 5, 2026). **[Reporting; low-quality secondary sources, verify with primary news]** — [ai-policy.org](https://ai-policy.org/the-companion-chatbot-reckoning-how-settlements-federal-inquiries-and-americas-first-companion-chatbot-law-are-reshaping-ai-emotional-care/); [AICompanionPick](https://www.aicompanionpick.com/character-ai-lawsuits-explained-2026)

### Inferences
- **The core risk for a connection app is becoming the relationship rather than routing to it.** The AI-companion literature is the starkest case of parasocial substitution. The same mechanism (comfort now, displacement later, worst for the already-isolated) applies to any app feature that satisfies the *feeling* of connection without the *act*: rich in-app feeds of friends' updates, AI "check-ins" that stand in for a call, or relationship dashboards that feel like maintenance.
- **If AI is used at all, make it a bridge, not a companion.** Good uses: drafting a message the user sends to a human, suggesting a time to call, summarizing what to ask a friend about. Avoid: personas, ongoing open-ended chat, emotional disclosure *to the bot* (Stanford found it associated with lower wellbeing), and any "don't go" farewell copy (De Freitas). Exit should always be one tap with neutral copy.
- **Streaks between people are a trap.** Mutual streaks convert friendship into an obligation ledger, and a missed day reads as social rejection (Snapchat evidence). If continuity cues are used, make them private, forgiving and repairable, framed around rhythm ("you two usually talk every couple of weeks") rather than consecutive-day counts. Never message users about a *broken* streak (Silverman).
- **No public counters of relationship activity** (friend scores, "most contacted," leaderboards). Etkin and the like-count debate suggest quantifying intrinsically motivated social acts can drain their meaning and invite comparison.
- **Avoid obligation-by-design (BeReal).** A daily prompt is a natural endpoint, which is good, but a *mandatory* daily ritual turns into a chore and a performance. Prompts should be optional and skippable without penalty or visible "misses."
- **Design for presence during the meetup.** The app's best behavior during an in-person meetup is to be absent: no notifications in that window, and possibly an automatic quiet mode tied to the calendar event (Dwyer et al.).

### Gaps
- No long-term *causal* RCT of AI-companion substitution (displacement of human contact) was found. The best evidence is longitudinal-observational (*Psychological Science* 2026) and short RCTs (MIT/OpenAI 4 weeks; De Freitas 1 week).
- No peer-reviewed study of Duolingo's streak effects (as opposed to company blog posts) was found this session. Duolingo's own streak data was not searched.
- The classic gamification-backfire literature (overjustification effect: Deci, Lepper; classroom gamification studies such as Hanus & Fox 2015) was not re-verified this session. Etkin is the closest verified analog.
- SB 243's full provision list (e.g., periodic break reminders to minors, reporting requirements) was not verified from the bill text. One low-quality tracker claims it bars engagement-maximizing notifications to minors, which could not be confirmed.

## 4. What design patterns support connection without getting in the way?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports: **predictable batched notifications** (about 3 per day beats both constant alerts and none); **friction against compulsive use** (grayscale, physical blockers, gradual stopping cues rather than one-off pop-ups, which users quickly tune out); **ambient, glanceable presence cues** (connectedness can arise without a message being sent); and **removing phones from in-person moments**. A key mechanism finding from Castelo et al. (2025) is that when mobile internet was blocked, people spent the reclaimed time **socializing in person**. That is the behavior a connection app should hand people off to. Most specific patterns (one-tap handoff, "free to talk" status, post-meetup check-ins) are well-motivated design inferences, but direct trials of them are scarce.

### Cited Findings

#### Notification batching
- Fitz, Kushlev, Jagannathan, Lewis, Paliwal & Ariely (*Computers in Human Behavior* 2019), randomized field experiment, n=237. Batching notifications **3 times a day** reduced stress and increased wellbeing. **Hourly** batching produced little change vs. control. **Turning notifications off entirely** increased anxiety and FoMO. The 3x/day group also reported more worry about missing out than control. **[Field exp.]** — [Springer Nature Communities](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/batching-notifications-can-improve-attention-well-being-and-productivity); [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953); [Georgia Tech Work21 summary](https://work21.gatech.edu/?p=402)

#### Stopping cues and gradual friction
- Meinhardt et al. (2026 preprint; N=104, 7-day field study on short-form video). A standard pop-up intervention was initially effective but quickly lost impact, while a *gradually intensifying visual friction* sustained its effect longest. Self-regulation traits (e.g., impulsivity) moderated which intervention worked. **[Field exp.; Preprint]** — [arXiv 2607.15818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15818)
- A 2025 preprint (N=72, 7 days) notes users become desensitized to interventions that lack contextual relevance. **[Field exp.; Preprint]** — [arXiv 2501.11814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11814)
- Rixen et al. (MobileHCI, award-winning) studied why people *leave* social media sessions (N=46, one-week field study) to inform interventions against infinite scrolling. **[Field study]** — [Ulm University news](https://uni-ulm.de/in/mi/institut/news/article/auszeichnung-an-rixen-et-al-mobilehci)

#### Friction that reduces compulsive use
- Zimmermann & Sobolev (pre-registered randomized field experiment, n=112). A **design-friction** intervention (grayscale) produced an immediate, significant drop in objectively measured screen time. **Goal-setting** produced a smaller, gradual drop. **Tracking alone** (control) produced no reduction. There was no immediate causal effect on subjective wellbeing or academic performance. **[RCT]** — [PubMed 36577008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36577008/); [IE repository](https://repositorio.ie.edu/entities/publication/78b87732-5c0d-494a-9769-bdfd4a501297/full)

#### Ambient awareness / presence signals
- Rettie argues *connectedness* is an emotional experience distinct from social presence. Awareness systems can create connectedness with very little social presence; for example, simply seeing a contact is online can make someone feel connected without any message exchange. **[Conceptual/empirical HCI]** — [Rettie (Kingston, PDF)](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/2106/1/Rettie.pdf)
- SnowGlobe (Visser, Vastenburg & Keyson) gave subtle ambient cues about activity in a person's social network. Interviews and logs suggested it increased relationship saliency and closeness. **[Field study/Qualitative]** — [International Journal of Design](https://ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/710)
- Gooch & Watts (21-day study, 63 participants) treat closeness and social presence as temporally distinct aspects of emotional connectedness when evaluating devices for personal relationships. **[Field study]** — [University of Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-impact-of-social-presence-on-feelings-of-closeness-in-persona/)
- "A Time to Glance": ambient mobile presence (motion presence, music sharing) maintained connectedness with close friends and family *with minimal interaction*. **[Field studies of prototypes]** — [CEUR-WS (PDF)](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-254/paper01.pdf)
- For older adults, indirect awareness of each other's activities can sustain close connections and reduce risks of isolation and loneliness. **[Academic chapter]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-10752-9_6)
- Calm Tech's "make use of the periphery" and "communicate without speaking" principles (e.g., light, haptics, glanceable states) are the design-philosophy counterpart. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [principles.design](https://principles.design/examples/principles-of-calm-technology)

#### Location sharing as a coordination primitive
- Among NYC college students, continuous location sharing (Life360, Find My) is used for **orchestrating social activities**, managing social membership and producing a sense of safety. New norms around self-disclosure, privacy and trust are emerging. **[Qualitative; CSCW 2026]** — [Drexel: "Like Guardian Angels"](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Like-Guardian-Angels-Continuous-Location-Sharing-and/991022175160604721)

#### Freed-up time goes to people (mechanism)
- Castelo et al. (*PNAS Nexus* 2025, preregistered RCT): with mobile internet blocked for 2 weeks (calls and texts still allowed), participants spent more time **socializing in person**, exercising and in nature, and wellbeing, mental health and sustained attention improved (details in section 6). **[RCT]** — [PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017)

#### Offline-outcome check-ins
- Hinge's "We Met" is a private post-date check-in that feeds the matching algorithm and the company's success metric (section 2). **[Company claim]** — [Latana](https://resources.latana.com/post/hinge-deep-dive/); [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2022/12/21/hinge-ceo-mcleod/)

#### Non-manipulative exits
- In the De Freitas audit, the wellness-focused app Flourish showed no manipulative farewells, which shows that clean exits are compatible with a companion-style product. **[Audit]** — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/10/28/harvard-researchers-just-documented-how-ai-emotionally-manipulates-people)

### Inferences
The following concrete pattern library is synthesized from the evidence above. Items are inferences unless they restate a cited finding.

1. **"Get in, get out."** Design the primary flow to finish in seconds: open, see who to reach, tap, and leave the app. Success is a *handoff* to native channels, never an in-app conversation. Use deep links to the phone dialer (`tel:`), SMS or iMessage (`sms:`), FaceTime or WhatsApp, calendar events (ICS / EventKit), and maps directions. Owning chat makes the app a destination.
2. **Zero-friction where connection happens, friction where compulsion happens.** Calling or texting should be one tap. Browsing, re-checking or re-nudging should carry friction, such as a cooldown before re-prompting about the same person, or no feed to scroll at all.
3. **Finite lists and natural endpoints.** Offer a small daily or weekly set ("3 people you might reach out to") and then an explicit done state ("That's it. Go live your life."). Never use an infinite list of suggestions.
4. **Predictable batched notifications at user-chosen times** (roughly 1–3 per day, or weekly), with no random timing and no urgency language. Don't remove notifications entirely, since Fitz et al. found that raised anxiety and FoMO, and the app's value depends on timely nudges.
5. **Ambient, opt-in presence instead of messaging pressure.** Use expiring "free to talk," "around this weekend" or "up for a walk" signals and a glanceable widget. Avoid read receipts, last-seen timestamps and typing indicators, which create response pressure. Make presence symmetric (you see others' status only if you share yours) and time-boxed by default.
6. **Gradual, contextual stopping cues** rather than one-off nag pop-ups, which users quickly tune out (Meinhardt 2026).
7. **Design for the moment after the app closes.** After a handoff, the app should go quiet. During scheduled meetups (from a calendar event), suppress all app notifications and optionally suggest a phone-down cue (Dwyer et al.). A later, single, skippable "Did you connect?" check-in closes the loop (We Met pattern).
8. **Intentional prompts tied to real-world hooks** instead of engagement bait: birthdays, a friend's stated "free" window, time since you last *saw* someone (privately framed), shared calendar gaps, or proximity when traveling to a friend's city (with consent).
9. **Forgiving rhythms, not streaks.** Use a private cadence goal per relationship ("monthly with Alex"), easy to snooze or pause, with no broken-streak messaging and no mutual or public counters (Silverman; Snapchat evidence).
10. **Be dumbphone- and Focus-compatible.** Users trying to cut screen time (section 6) are the target audience. Core nudges should degrade to SMS or email digests, and the app should respect OS Focus/Do Not Disturb modes rather than fight them.

### Gaps
- No controlled studies were found on "free to talk"/availability indicators (e.g., Apple Focus status sharing, Messages "Check In," Find My) and their effect on how often people actually call or meet.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation was found of handoff-first "personal CRM"/friendship-nudging apps measuring offline contact as an outcome.
- Evidence on ideal nudge frequency for *relationship* reminders, as opposed to general notification batching, was not found.

## 5. Privacy, consent, and trust considerations for relationship data (contacts, location, relationship notes / "personal CRM")

### Takeaway
Relationship data is inherently about **people who aren't the user** (VSD's indirect stakeholders). Research on young people's location sharing shows it is normalized and valued for coordination and safety, but it also carries emerging norms, social-membership signaling and cross-cultural differences in privacy concern. The industry record is poor: Life360 was reported to sell location data to brokers. Trust requires data minimization, symmetry, reversibility and never monetizing the social graph. Peer-reviewed research specifically on "personal CRM" creepiness was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Continuous location sharing among college students** (Life360, Find My; CSCW 2026, "Like Guardian Angels"): it is used to orchestrate social activities, manage social membership (who is "in" your sharing circle signals closeness), and produce a sense of safety. New norms around self-disclosure, privacy and trust-building are emerging. **[Qualitative]** — [Drexel Research Discovery](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Like-Guardian-Angels-Continuous-Location-Sharing-and/991022175160604721)
- **Cross-national differences:** in 21 interviews, Japanese youth primarily used location-sharing platforms to strengthen friendships, while UK youth emphasized personal safety, expressed higher privacy concerns, and often avoided constant activation. **[Qualitative; conference paper, ISA Forum 2025]** — [ISA Forum 2025](https://isaconf.confex.com/isaconf/forum2025/webprogram/Paper152337.html)
- **University of Illinois study** (*Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*): respondents shared location with nearly four people on average, most often romantic partners, friends and family. Motives were safety, practicality, casual use and **relationship management**. **[Observational]** — [Chambana Today](https://chambanatoday.com/?p=41068)
- Life360's own survey reports 94% of Gen Z say location sharing benefits their lives. **[Company claim; vendor survey]** — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/gen-z-endorses-location-sharing-associates-it-with-safety/)
- In 2022 *The Markup* reported Life360 was selling users' location data to as many as a dozen data brokers. **[Reporting, via secondary source]** — [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/gen-z-endorses-location-sharing-associates-it-with-safety/); [The Lead (surveillance-culture critique)](https://national.thelead.uk/p/location-sharing-surveillance-culture-genz)
- Commentators frame mass friend location-sharing among Gen Z as "becoming our own spies," a normalization of peer surveillance. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [The Lead](https://national.thelead.uk/p/location-sharing-surveillance-culture-genz)
- Zenly, a social-map app popular with students and young adults, shut down, which reportedly left a gap in the friend-location market. **[Reporting; low-quality source]** — [WeAreOpen discourse](https://discourse.weareopen.coop/news/zenly-app-what-is-it)
- VSD requires explicitly considering **indirect stakeholders**, people affected by a system who don't use it. **[Academic framework]** — [Friedman, Kahn & Borning (PDF)](https://vsd.ccs.neu.edu/introduction/resources.files/Value_Sensitive_Design.pdf)
- Calm Tech's "respect social norms" principle advises considering which norms a technology might violate and introducing features gradually. **[Advocacy/opinion]** — [caseorganic.com](https://www.caseorganic.com/post/principles-of-calm-technology)
- Disclosure of sensitive personal information *to chatbots* correlated with lower wellbeing (Stanford 2026). This is relevant if relationship notes or journaling are processed by AI. **[Observational]** — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1138850)
- Younger teens (13–14) were more likely than older teens to trust AI companions' advice (Common Sense Media 2025). **[Observational survey]** — [ScienceAlert](https://sciencealert.com/almost-75-of-american-teens-have-used-ai-companions-study-finds)

### Inferences
- **Treat friends' data as borrowed, not owned.** Never bulk-upload contact lists to a server. Do contact matching on-device or with hashed, opt-in discovery. Store relationship notes ("Sam's mom is sick," "Alex hates phone calls") **locally or end-to-end encrypted** by default. Never use them for ads, inference products or model training.
- **The "creepiness gradient":** a feature feels creepy when the *other person* would be surprised to learn about it. Private reminders ("you wanted to check in with Sam") are low-creep. Inferred scores ("Sam is drifting away, relationship health 42%") and passive tracking (who you were near, how often you text) are high-creep, even when accurate. Prefer user-entered intentions over inferred surveillance.
- **Presence and location must be symmetric, granular, time-boxed and quietly reversible.** Share "in my neighborhood this weekend" or "free tonight" rather than continuous GPS. Expire by default. Allow pause without notifying others, since signaling who is and isn't in your circle carries social-membership weight (CSCW 2026). Show clearly who can see what.
- **No social-graph monetization.** Life360's history is the cautionary tale. A connection app's trust is its product, and a privacy breach of relationship notes would be uniquely damaging.
- **Cultural and age variation** (Japan vs. UK; younger teens' higher AI trust) argues for conservative defaults, especially for minors: location off by default and no AI-mediated emotional features.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed research was found on perceptions of "personal CRM" apps (e.g., Dex, Clay, Monica) or the creepiness of keeping notes on friends. This is a real evidence gap.
- No Find My–specific peer-reviewed study and no research on Zenly's "ghost mode"-type features were retrieved. Pew or similar national data on young people's location sharing was not found this session.
- Legal specifics (GDPR treatment of third-party contact data, CCPA, Apple/Google contact-API policies) were not researched.

## 6. What does the evidence say on digital wellbeing tools (screen-time limits, grayscale, blockers, dumbphones like Light Phone, Brick, Opal)?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is the **Castelo et al. 2025 PNAS Nexus RCT**: blocking mobile internet for two weeks improved wellbeing, mental health and sustained attention, with 91% improving on at least one outcome. The mechanism appears to be more time with people, exercise and nature. **Friction-based** interventions (grayscale, blockers) reliably *reduce screen time*, while **monitoring alone does not**. Wellbeing benefits of milder interventions are less consistent; one RCT found no immediate wellbeing effect. Dumbphones (Light Phone III) and physical blockers (Brick) are a growing consumer trend backed mostly by reviews and company claims, with no controlled trials found.

### Cited Findings
- **Castelo, Kushlev, Ward, Esterman & Reiner, *PNAS Nexus* (Feb 2025),** preregistered RCT with 467 recruited participants. Mobile internet (data and Wi-Fi on the phone) was blocked for 2 weeks while calls, texts and desktop internet remained. A delayed-intervention group served as control. Blocking reduced smartphone use and improved subjective wellbeing (life satisfaction, positive affect), mental health ("more than antidepressants," the authors' comparison) and sustained attention ("as much as being 10 years younger," the authors' framing). **91% improved on at least one outcome.** Participants spent more time socializing in person, exercising and in nature. Press coverage reports benefits persisted after access was restored. **[RCT]** — [PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250219/Blocking-mobile-internet-for-two-weeks-improves-mental-health-and-well-being.aspx); [Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/health_science/article/bymboqfble)
  - Caveats: compliance was imperfect (a summary reports only 266 of 467 set up the blocking app; verify against the paper), and participants knew the study was about smartphone use (possible demand effects). — [search-summary caveats; verify in PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/2/pgaf017/8016017)
- **Zimmermann & Sobolev,** randomized field experiment (n=112). Grayscale/design friction gave an immediate, significant screen-time reduction. Goal-setting gave a smaller, gradual reduction. Tracking-only controls did *not* reduce screen time. There was **no immediate causal effect on subjective wellbeing** or academic performance. **[RCT]** — [PubMed 36577008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36577008/)
- A separate within-person grayscale study (84 participants; one baseline week, one grayscale week) reported reduced screen time and improved digital wellbeing. **[Within-subject, non-randomized; source attribution from search summary, verify]** — [Mobile Media & Communication (SAGE)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20501579231212062)
- **Notification batching** (Fitz et al. 2019): 3x/day batching improved wellbeing, and turning notifications off entirely increased anxiety and FoMO (section 4). **[Field exp.]** — [Springer Nature Communities](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/batching-notifications-can-improve-attention-well-being-and-productivity)
- **Pop-up limits decay:** one-off pop-up interventions lose effect within days, while gradual friction holds up better (2026 preprint). **[Field exp.; Preprint]** — [arXiv 2607.15818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15818)
- **OS-level tools:** Apple Screen Time and Android Digital Wellbeing are credited as outgrowths of the Time Well Spent movement. **[Reporting; causal attribution is the source's claim]** — [Wikipedia: Tristan Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Harris)
- **Light Phone III:** retails at $699. It blocks unrestricted app stores and web browsers but includes tools such as maps and an authenticator. Some carriers are incompatible (e.g., Google Fi, Cricket, Metro). The cofounder says over 100,000 people use the company's devices worldwide. **[Reporting; user count is a company claim]** — [Wikipedia: Light Phone III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Phone_III); [Vice review](https://www.vice.com/en/via/light-phone-iii-review/); [AOL/CNN](https://www.aol.com/articles/dumbphones-getting-smarter-more-premium-081502581.html)
- Minimalist phones are described as "mainstream" in 2026 as adults seek relief from infinite feeds. Practical friction points are banking apps and two-factor codes. **[Reporting/opinion; consumer guides]** — [DesignWhine 2026](https://www.designwhine.com/best-dumb-phones-minimalist-phones-2026/); [PauseGadget 2026](https://pausegadget.com/best-dumbphones/)
- **Brick:** a $59 NFC tag that locks selected apps until the phone is physically tapped to it, often kept away from the user (e.g., on the fridge) to add friction. It includes an "Emergency Unbrick" usable five times. Individual reviewers at Consumer Reports and the Irish Times reported meaningful reductions, roughly halving screen time. **No controlled study found.** **[Anecdotal reviews]** — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics-computers/cell-phones/can-brick-solve-my-screen-time-problem-a3152096219); [Irish Times, Nov 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/technology/consumer-tech/review/2025/11/13/brick-review-this-simple-device-helped-me-halve-my-screen-time); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/brick-review)
- **Opal:** a software blocker costing about $99.99/yr. It claims to have saved users more than 220 million hours **[Company claim]**. Tom's Guide argues its in-app unblocking "doesn't create that much of a barrier" compared with Brick's physical friction **[Opinion]**. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/brick-review)

### Inferences
- **Friction works for reducing use, but wellbeing gains depend on what replaces the screen time.** Castelo's wellbeing gains came with more in-person socializing. Zimmermann & Sobolev reduced screen time without immediate wellbeing gains. A plausible synthesis: reclaimed time improves wellbeing when it flows to relationships and embodied activity. **That is precisely the gap a connection app can fill.** Position it as "what to do with the time you just got back," and partner with or complement blockers rather than compete for attention.
- **Monitoring alone doesn't change behavior** (tracking-only control showed no reduction). A "time with friends" dashboard by itself is unlikely to increase connection. Concrete, timely prompts plus easy handoff are more likely to work.
- **Physical and commitment friction beats self-negotiated limits** (Brick vs. Opal anecdotes; pop-up decay). For a connection app, the analog is to make the right action trivially easy (one-tap call) rather than nag users to stop doing the wrong thing.
- **Design for the dumbphone and Focus-mode user:** SMS-based nudges, email digests and calendar-invite handoffs let the product work for people who've deliberately de-smartened their phones, a growing, motivated segment.
- Effects are modest and studies are short, self-selected and partly self-reported. Claims like "better than antidepressants" are author framing, not head-to-head comparisons.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis of grayscale or time-limit interventions was found. No long-term (more than 3 months) RCT of mobile-internet blocking or dumbphone adoption was found.
- No controlled studies of Light Phone, Brick or Opal exist in the retrieved results. Usage and impact numbers are company claims or individual reviews.
- No independent data was found on the effectiveness of Apple Screen Time/Downtime, Focus modes, or Google Digital Wellbeing on wellbeing outcomes.
- 2026 market-size or sales data for dumbphones was not found beyond the Light Phone cofounder's 100k-user claim.
